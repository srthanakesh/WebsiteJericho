Age Well · Product story · Business showcase

More confidence for the wearer. More reassurance for the people who care.

Project Jericho is an AI cognitive and sensory companion designed specifically for older adults. This document defines how the website can communicate its purpose, current prototype, and future potential to older adults, families, caretakers, judges, partners, and care organizations.

Human-first Caretaker-aware Prototype-led Trustworthy by design

01 · Context

A business-facing product showcase

The website is being developed in the context of the hackathon theme Age Well. It should communicate the product's purpose, demonstrate the current prototype, build trust, and make the opportunity understandable.

Jericho helps older adults stay more independent and connected, while giving the people who care about them more reassurance.

This is not primarily a technical documentation page and not a direct-to-consumer ecommerce page yet. It is an emotional, credible, business-oriented introduction to the product and its potential.

02 · Audience

One product, two human perspectives

Older adults

  • Simple and familiar to use.
  • Respectful and non-patronizing.
  • Helpful during ordinary moments at home.
  • Designed to support independence, not replace it.

Families and caretakers

  • Understand what Jericho can help with today.
  • See how it reduces everyday friction and cognitive load.
  • Understand its potential for safer medication and object routines.
  • Feel reassured without feeling that care has become surveillance.

Judges, partners, and care organizations

They should see a clear social problem, a technically credible solution, working functionality, a realistic path from prototype to product, and awareness of safety, consent, privacy, and accessibility.

Important: The older adult should be portrayed as active, capable, and socially connected—with extra support available when needed.

03 · Positioning

Support independence. Reassure care.

Jericho is not about adding more technology to an older person's life. It is about making everyday technology feel less visible while helping the person stay connected to the world around them.

Language directions

  • “A little more confidence, every day.”
  • “Stay connected to the moment.”
  • “Support for independence. Reassurance for care.”
  • “Technology that helps people stay themselves.”

Tone

Warm, clear, calm, and dignified. Avoid language that implies the wearer is incapable or that Jericho replaces family, professional caregivers, or medical professionals.

04 · Inspiration

Borrow the rhythm, make the story ours

Our current visual and storytelling inspiration is Even Realities.

What is compelling

  • Feeling and lifestyle before specification sheets.
  • Large cinematic visual moments.
  • Features shown as real situations.
  • Technology framed as quiet and naturally integrated.
  • Clear chapters for promise, use cases, proof, and technology.

What Jericho should make distinct

  • The story is about aging well, not performance.
  • The main character is an older adult and their support network.
  • Caretakers and family members are a core audience.
  • The emotional outcome is dignity, connection, confidence, and independence.

Use the reference site's storytelling principles without copying its wording, layouts, visual identity, or claims.

05 · Scroll narrative

Product → inside the product → everyday life → proof → reassurance

The page should feel like one continuous story, moving from “what is it?” to “how does it work?” to “who does it help?”

0
Opening promiseEstablish the human reason for the product before introducing features. Possible direction: “Staying independent should feel natural.”
1
The glasses by themselvesWhite or warm off-white background. Glasses centered like familiar everyday eyewear. Very little text and slow movement.
2
The product opensThe glasses rotate, separate, and reveal only the important internal components: vision, voice, memory, processing, and power.
3
The glasses reassembleThe animation reverses. Parts move back into place, labels fade away, and the product becomes visually simple again.
4
From product to personThe glasses move toward an older adult's face. The clean product space becomes a warm home. Help is available without taking control away.
5
Real functionalityCurrent videos become evidence of the product's abilities, organized around simple human questions rather than shown as a detached gallery.
6
The two-sided valueShow the benefit for the wearer and the reassurance for the caretaker side by side.
7
Closing invitationEnd with a calm product shot and an invitation to learn, see the demonstration, partner, or help build the future of aging well.

Human scene direction

An AI presence can appear subtly as a soft light, calm interface layer, conversational orb, or visual thread connecting the wearer to useful information.

The support network should not suddenly appear from dark shadows, since that may feel ominous. Reveal family members or caretakers as people already present in the home environment, gradually becoming visible as the story expands.

The wearer remains in control. Help is available when needed. Care can feel connected even when people are not in the same room.

Functionality video framing

See it

Can Jericho identify what is in front of me?

Find it

Can Jericho help me remember where something was?

Read it

Can Jericho read a label, letter, or small print aloud?

Ask it

Can I speak naturally and receive help?

Be explicit: Separate current prototype demonstrations from future product features and anything that would require additional caregiver infrastructure.

06 · Current prototype

Explain the human benefit before the technical system

  • Camera-based identification of everyday objects and medicines.
  • Voice interaction and conversation.
  • Live room memory for finding where objects were last seen.
  • Medication-related memory and safety reminders.
  • Reading letters, labels, bills, and small print aloud.
  • Object tracking and re-identification powered by the REMIND system.

Use plain language on the main page. Technical terms such as REMIND, object re-identification, visual memory, and DINO features can appear later in a “How it works” or “Technology” section for judges and partners.

07 · Visual direction

Premium but warm, calm rather than flashy

Overall feel

  • Human rather than clinical.
  • Spacious rather than dense.
  • Trustworthy without feeling like a hospital or insurance website.
  • Cinematic, but grounded in ordinary life.

Color progression

  1. Warm white and soft grey for the product reveal.
  2. Charcoal, graphite, and restrained electric blue for internals.
  3. Warm cream, amber, and muted green for home and human scenes.
  4. Darker media panels for functionality.
  5. Warm white again for the closing message.

Motion principles

  • Slow and deliberate product motion.
  • One major visual idea per scroll chapter.
  • Text appears when the visual has earned it.
  • The story still works if motion is reduced or disabled.
  • The caretaker should never feel like a surveillance operator.

08 · Asset strategy

Prototype the story before investing in the final model

First prototype: SVG / CSS

  • Fast to build and easy to change.
  • Lightweight and responsive.
  • Supports rotation, separation, glow, labels, and scroll-driven transitions.
  • Useful while product details are still evolving.

Later: Blender product animation

  1. Model the glasses once the design is known.
  2. Animate opening, explosion, and reassembly.
  3. Render with transparent or controlled backgrounds.
  4. Use a scroll-controlled image sequence or optimized video.

The SVG prototype should use the same scene structure as the future Blender animation, so the asset can be replaced later without redesigning the page.

09 · Business trust

More than a cinematic hero

Because this is a business-facing website, the experience should eventually include sections that make the project credible to families, care organizations, partners, and judges.

The problem

Everyday cognitive and sensory challenges faced by older adults.

The solution

Jericho as a companion that helps with seeing, remembering, reading, and asking.

How it works

Simple system explanation, followed by optional technical depth.

Caregiver perspective

Why this matters to families and care teams.

Prototype evidence

Current videos and measurable demonstrations.

Privacy and consent

What the system sees, stores, and shares—and what remains under the user's control.

Safety boundaries

The product supports daily life but does not replace medical professionals or human caregivers.

Roadmap and partnership

Future care collaboration, richer home memory, and a clear path for organizations and collaborators.

Trust matters: Privacy and consent should be explained proactively, especially for an elderly-focused product.

10 · Boundaries

Supportive independence, not fear-based care

The website should not:

  • Suggest that Jericho diagnoses medical conditions.
  • Imply that the product can guarantee safety.
  • Suggest that it replaces caregivers or family relationships.
  • Present future features as currently available.
  • Use fear-based imagery about aging.
  • Frame older adults as passive recipients of care.
  • Imply that continuous monitoring is required for the product to be useful.

The strongest business position is supportive independence: Jericho helps the wearer do more while helping their support network feel more connected.

11 · Open decisions

Questions to settle before the final visual system

Product and brand
  • Final product name: Jericho, REMIND Glasses, or another name.
  • Physical appearance of the glasses.
  • Whether the product includes a visible display, camera, speakers, or other components.
Experience and claims
  • Which caregiver features are available now versus planned.
  • Which existing videos are strongest for the public-facing story.
  • Privacy and consent model.
  • Primary call to action for the hackathon presentation.
Future website
  • Whether the visual style should be realistic, illustrated, or a blend.
  • Whether the site remains a single scrolling presentation or becomes a broader product website.

12 · Recommended next step

Storyboard first. Model second.

  1. Create a low-fidelity page with the seven narrative chapters.
  2. Use placeholder blocks for the glasses, human scene, and videos.
  3. Write one sentence of copy per chapter.
  4. Review the distinction between wearer value and caretaker value.
  5. Only then invest in Blender modeling or detailed visual assets.

Working principle: Validate the story first. Once the narrative and product claims are stable, upgrade the SVG prototype to a Blender-rendered product animation.
